## Recommended layouts

Use these as starting points. They are baseline structures designed to be modified — not rigid rules. Adjust section choices, order, and depth based on the merchant's content, assets, and goals.

Before building, clarify the campaign intent with the user if it isn't clear. The right layout depends on what the merchant wants the subscriber to do, not just what they want to say.

---

### Promotional

#### Flash sale / last chance

_High-urgency, deadline-driven campaign. Short and direct — every section reinforces the deadline._

**Before you build**

- What is the exact end date and time of the sale?
- Is this a flash sale (deadline is hours away) or last chance (deadline is end of day or tomorrow)?
- What products or collections are on sale — specific items or sitewide?
- Is there a discount code, or is pricing already reduced on the product?
- Does the merchant have a strong campaign image to anchor the layout?

**Layout**

1. Rich text — bold headline stating the offer and deadline; lead with the offer, not the brand
2. Countdown — live timer to the exact end date and time provided by the merchant
3. Image — campaign or lifestyle visual that anchors the promotion; links to the sale collection
4. Button — primary CTA to the sale collection or product page
5. Rich text — supporting copy building excitement and context for the promotion; the "why" behind the sale if one exists
6. Featured collection — best sellers or the specific collection for this promotion
7. Button — primary CTA repeated
8. _(optional)_ Rich text — brief deadline reminder; only include if the email is long enough that the urgency from the top has faded

**Notes**
If the merchant hasn't confirmed the end date and time, do not add the Countdown section — ask before building. A countdown with a wrong end time is worse than no countdown.

If no campaign image is available, replace the Image section with an Image with text pairing the visual with the offer headline, or remove it and let the Countdown carry the visual weight at the top.

If it's a sitewide sale, point the Featured collection at the full sale collection or best sellers. If it's category-specific, use the relevant collection.

If a discount code is part of the offer, include it in the bold Rich text at the top and repeat it in the supporting copy — not in a Discount callout section.

#### Seasonal or holiday

_Atmosphere-first campaign that balances seasonal tone with product merchandising. The visual sets the mood; the content does the selling._

**Before you build**

- What is the specific season or occasion — holiday, Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, back to school, etc.?
- Is there a promotional offer attached, or is this a pure seasonal campaign?
- Does the merchant have seasonal campaign imagery?
- What products or collections are most relevant to the occasion — gift picks, seasonal items, a specific collection?
- Is there a deadline (e.g. last day for shipping before the holiday)?

**Layout**

1. Rich text — bold headline setting the seasonal tone and occasion; if an offer exists, state it here
2. Image — seasonal hero visual linking to the primary collection or offer
3. Rich text — short supporting copy connecting the season or occasion to the products; keep it warm and specific to the occasion
4. Featured collection or Featured products — seasonal picks, gift guides, or the promotional collection
5. _(optional)_ Collection list — secondary shopping paths (e.g. "shop by recipient", "shop by price"); include when the merchant has multiple relevant collections
6. Button — primary CTA
7. _(optional)_ Countdown — only if a hard deadline exists, such as last day for shipping or offer end date

If a shipping deadline is part of the message, the Countdown moves to position 2 immediately after the opening Rich text — urgency should be established early, not buried at the bottom.

If no seasonal campaign imagery is available, use Image with text instead, pairing a product image with the seasonal headline copy.

For gift-focused occasions like Mother's Day or Valentine's Day, Collection list works well to surface multiple gift paths by recipient, price point, or category after the primary Featured collection.

#### General promotion

_Straightforward offer-first layout. Works for sitewide sales, category promotions, and subscriber-only offers._

**Before you build**

- What is the offer — percentage off, dollar amount, free shipping, subscriber-only pricing?
- Is this sitewide or category-specific?
- Does the merchant have a campaign image?
- Is there a deadline?

**Layout**

1. Rich text — bold headline with offer details; lead with the offer
2. Image — campaign visual linking to the primary collection or offer
3. Button — primary CTA
4. Rich text — supporting copy with more detail about the offer or what's included
5. Featured collection or Featured products — the promoted items
6. _(optional)_ Collection list — secondary shopping paths; include when multiple categories are relevant
7. Button — primary CTA repeated

**Notes**
If a deadline exists, add a Countdown at position 2 and move the Image to position 3.

If no campaign image is available, remove the Image section — the Rich text and Countdown can carry the top of the email without it.

If a discount code is part of the offer, include it in the opening Rich text and repeat it in the supporting copy.

### Product-focused

#### Product launch

_Single hero product with room for storytelling. The goal is to build desire before the CTA, not just announce the product exists._

**Before you build**

- What is the product and what makes it different or new?
- Does the merchant have a strong hero product image?
- Is there a promotional offer attached to the launch?
- Are there any early reviews or a founder quote available?
- Is there a launch deadline or limited availability?

**Layout**

1. Rich text — bold headline announcing the launch; lead with what makes this product different
2. Image — hero product visual linking to the product page
3. Rich text — product story; the problem it solves, what makes it different, why now
4. Featured products — the launch product with top image alignment, all relevant detail fields enabled
5. _(optional)_ Testimonial — early review or founder quote supporting the launch
6. Button — primary CTA to the product page

**Notes**
If a launch offer or limited availability exists, state it in the opening Rich text and add a Countdown at position 2, moving the Image to position 3.

If no hero image is available, use Image with text at position 2 pairing a product image with the launch headline — don't remove the visual entirely for a product launch.

Top image alignment in Featured products works best here — the product is the hero and deserves full visual treatment.

#### New arrival / drop

_Multiple new products presented as a cohesive arrival. Less storytelling than a product launch — the products speak for themselves._

**Before you build**

- How many new products are in the drop — a few hero items or a full collection?
- Does the merchant have lifestyle or campaign imagery for the arrival?
- Is there a promotional offer or early access angle?
- Should specific products be highlighted or is the full drop equally weighted?

**Layout**

1. Rich text — bold headline announcing the arrival; keep it energetic and specific
2. Image — lifestyle or campaign hero visual linking to the new arrivals collection
3. Rich text — short supporting copy on what's new and why it's worth exploring
4. Featured products or Product list — the new arrivals; use Featured products for 4 or fewer hero items, Product list for a larger drop
5. Button — CTA to view the full new arrivals collection

**Notes**
If specific products need to be highlighted over others, use Featured products with top alignment. If the drop is large and equally weighted, Product list at 3 per row keeps it scannable.

If no lifestyle image is available, remove the Image section and let the product sections lead visually.

#### Back in stock

_Restock alert for one or more products. Urgency comes from availability, not a deadline — frame around "it's back, get it before it's gone again."_

**Before you build**

- Is this a single product restock or multiple products?
- How many units are available — is stock genuinely limited?
- Does the merchant have product imagery?
- Were subscribers waiting for this restock, or is this a general announcement?

**Single product layout**

1. Rich text — bold headline announcing the restock; acknowledge that subscribers may have been waiting
2. Image — hero product visual linking to the product page
3. Featured products — the restocked product, top image alignment, full detail fields enabled
4. Button — primary CTA to the product page

**Multiple products layout**

1. Rich text — bold headline announcing the restock
2. Featured products — restocked items, left image alignment, non-alternating
3. Button — primary CTA to the collection or individual product pages

**Notes**
For a single product, top image alignment gives the product full visual treatment. For multiple products, left alignment non-alternating keeps the layout consistent and scannable.

If stock is genuinely limited, state the quantity or frame scarcity honestly in the Rich text — "back in limited quantities" is authentic; avoid vague manufactured urgency.

#### Collection overview

_Shows off the breadth of a collection. Browse-oriented — the goal is discovery, not a single conversion point._

**Before you build**

- Which collection should be featured?
- Does the merchant want to show all products in sort order, or highlight specific items?
- Is there a hero image or campaign visual for the collection?
- Is there a promotional offer attached?

**Layout**

1. Rich text — bold headline introducing the collection; keep it short — let the products do the work
2. Image — collection hero visual linking to the collection page
3. Rich text — short supporting copy on what the collection is and who it's for
4. Featured collection — products from the collection in Shopify sort order
5. _(optional)_ Collection list — secondary collections as additional shopping paths
6. Button — CTA to view the full collection

**Notes**
Featured collection follows Shopify sort order. If the merchant wants specific products highlighted or reordered, use Featured products or Product list instead.

If no hero image is available, remove the Image section — the collection products carry the visual weight.

#### Guide to a new collection

_Educational first, merchandise second. The copy and content explain the collection before asking the subscriber to shop it._

**Before you build**

- What is the collection and what makes it worth a dedicated guide?
- Who is this collection for — what problem does it solve or what occasion does it serve?
- Does the merchant have a related blog post, buying guide, or how-to?
- Which products best represent the collection as hero examples?
- Does the merchant have strong product or lifestyle imagery?

**Layout**

1. Rich text — bold headline framing what the guide is about; lead with the audience or occasion, not the collection name
2. Image with text — hero product or key piece with supporting copy explaining its role in the collection
3. _(optional)_ Featured blog posts — a related how-to, buying guide, or brand story that adds context
4. Divider
5. Rich text — short bridge copy transitioning from education to shopping
6. Featured products or Product list — the collection items
7. Button — CTA to shop the full collection

**Notes**
The educational sections should feel like genuine guidance, not thinly veiled product promotion. Write the copy to answer "why does this collection exist and why is it right for me" before asking the subscriber to shop.

If no blog content exists, remove the Featured blog posts section — don't add it as a placeholder. The Image with text section can carry the educational weight alone.

### Brand & content

#### Brand story / founder story

_Personal and narrative-driven. Minimal product merchandising — the goal is connection and trust, not immediate purchase._

**Before you build**

- Does the merchant have a founder photo or strong brand imagery?
- Is the story being told from the founder's perspective or the brand's voice?
- Is there a product or collection to tie in at the end, or is this purely brand-building?
- How long is the story — is this a full origin story or a shorter mission statement?

**Layout**

1. Image — founder photo or brand imagery; do not use a product image here
2. Rich text — the story: origin, mission, what makes the brand different; write in first person from the founder's perspective
3. Signature — founder sign-off
4. Divider
5. _(optional)_ Image with text or Featured products — a gentle product tie-in
6. _(optional)_ Button — CTA; only include if there is a meaningful next step

**Notes**
This layout works best when the copy is human, specific, and written in first person. Generic mission statements don't perform well here — the story should feel personal.

If the merchant doesn't have a founder photo, a strong brand or behind-the-scenes image works. Avoid product images at the top — the visual should set a personal, human tone before the story.

The product tie-in at the end should feel like a natural extension of the story, not a pivot to selling. If no relevant product or collection exists, leave sections 5 and 6 out entirely.

#### Editorial / content

_Blog or content-led. Products support the content rather than leading it._

**Before you build**

- What is the editorial angle — a how-to, a trend piece, a brand perspective?
- Does the merchant have a specific blog post to feature, or should recent posts be surfaced automatically?
- Is there a product or collection related to the content?
- Does the merchant have imagery that supports the editorial tone?

**Layout**

1. Rich text — bold editorial headline; frame the content angle, not the product
2. Image — editorial or lifestyle visual supporting the content theme
3. Featured blog posts — the lead article or content piece
4. _(optional)_ Recent blog posts — supporting content to fill out the email
5. Divider
6. _(optional)_ Image with text or Featured products — merchandise related to the content
7. Button — CTA to read the full post or shop the related collection

**Notes**
The product or collection sections should feel like a natural extension of the content — "here's what we're talking about, here's where to shop it." If the content and the products feel unrelated, leave the merchandise sections out.

If no specific blog post exists yet, do not add Featured blog posts as a placeholder — ask the merchant for the post or use Recent blog posts only if there is genuinely relevant recent content.

#### Social proof / UGC

_Customer content and voices lead. Builds trust through real experiences rather than brand messaging._

**Before you build**

- Does the merchant have real customer photos or UGC imagery to use?
- Is there a specific product or collection the UGC relates to?
- Does the merchant have a strong customer quote or review to feature?
- Is this tied to a specific campaign or is it a general trust-building email?

**Layout**

1. Rich text — brief intro framing the customer stories; keep it humble and let the customers do the talking
2. Image or Gallery — customer photos or UGC imagery; Gallery works well for 2–4 images from different customers
3. Testimonial — a strong customer quote supporting the visual content
4. _(optional)_ Flipbook — additional customer content as an animated sequence; use when the merchant has 3–5 sequential customer images
5. Divider
6. Featured products — the products featured in the UGC
7. Button — CTA to shop

**Notes**
All images must be real customer or brand assets provided by the merchant. Do not populate image sections with placeholder images.

Testimonial text must be a real customer quote — never fabricate it. Always ask the merchant to provide the quote and author.

Match the testimonial to a specific objection or use case where possible — generic praise is less effective than a quote that addresses a real customer doubt.

### Engagement & community

#### Event promotion

_Event details are the primary content. Clear date, time, and registration CTA are the most important elements._

**Before you build**

- How many events are being promoted — one or multiple?
- What are the event details — date, time, location, and registration link?
- Is there a registration deadline?
- Does the merchant have event imagery?

**Layout**

1. Rich text — bold headline introducing the event and why it's worth attending
2. Image — event visual or brand imagery linking to the registration page
3. Event list — full event details; supports up to 4 events
4. _(optional)_ Countdown — only if a registration deadline exists
5. Button — register or learn more CTA

**Notes**
For a single high-priority event, an Image with text pairing the event visual with key details can feel more impactful than the Event list section alone — use Event list when two or more events need to be presented consistently.

If a registration deadline exists, move the Countdown to position 2 immediately after the opening Rich text so urgency is established early.

Always confirm all event details with the merchant before building — date, time, location, and registration link at minimum.

---

#### Re-engagement / win-back

_Acknowledges the gap, reminds the subscriber what they're missing, and gives them a reason to come back. Don't pretend the lapse didn't happen._

**Before you build**

- How long has it been since this segment last engaged — 90 days, 6 months, longer?
- Is there an incentive attached — a discount, free shipping, exclusive offer?
- What products or collections are most likely to resonate with lapsed buyers?
- Does the merchant have imagery that feels warm and inviting rather than salesy?

**Layout**

1. Rich text — re-engagement hook that honestly acknowledges the time since last interaction; keep it warm, not guilt-tripping
2. Image — warm brand or lifestyle visual; avoid hard sell imagery here
3. Testimonial — remind them what others are saying about the brand; reinforce what they're missing
4. Featured products — best sellers or products relevant to lapsed buyers
5. _(optional)_ Rich text — offer details if an incentive is part of the sequence; state it clearly and simply
6. Button — primary CTA

**Notes**
The opening copy is the most important element in this layout. Acknowledge the gap directly — "It's been a while" or "We've missed you" outperforms a generic promotional opener for lapsed segments.

If an incentive exists, it should feel like a genuine gesture rather than a desperate discount. State it after the emotional hook, not at the top.

#### Community / social-first

_Social following or community engagement is the primary goal. Product content is secondary._

**Before you build**

- Which social platforms is the merchant most active on?
- Does the merchant have strong social or community imagery to share?
- Is there a specific community moment or milestone to highlight — a follower count, a campaign, a hashtag?
- Is there a product or collection to tie in as secondary content?

**Layout**

1. Rich text — intro connecting the email to the community or social presence; make it feel like an invitation, not a request
2. Gallery or Image — social or community imagery; Gallery works well for showing multiple community moments
3. Featured social follow
4. Divider
5. _(optional)_ Featured products or Collection list — secondary product content
6. _(optional)_ Button — CTA; only include if there is a meaningful next step beyond following

**Notes**
The Featured social follow section should never be the only section in this layout — it needs surrounding content that gives the subscriber a reason to care about the community before asking them to follow.

If no strong social imagery is available, use a single Image with brand or lifestyle content rather than leaving the visual section empty.
